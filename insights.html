Ideas that shape the work

Make learning meaningful beyond the room.

A few practice notes on designing learning around human behavior, the work people do and what an organization needs to change.

01 · Learning design

Practice before the program ends

People need a chance to try an idea while the learning is still supported. A useful session makes room for practice, feedback and reflection alongside new information.

Read the practice note

Role-play, group problem solving and short workplace scenarios help connect concepts to decisions participants will make later. The activity should fit the learning outcome; activity for its own sake adds little.

02 · Leadership

Build around the situations managers face

Leadership development becomes more concrete when participants can bring real conversations, priorities and team challenges into the room.

Read the practice note

Start with the responsibilities and relationships managers actually hold. Then build in time to reflect on choices, practice a different response and name one application to carry back to work.

03 · Communication

Influence starts with listening

Clear communication depends on what the other person needs to understand, decide or do. The message is only one part of the interaction.

Read the practice note

Exercises in listening, reframing and narrative can help people notice how intent, context and behavior affect a conversation. Participants can then practice adapting their approach to the audience and situation.

04 · Organizational learning

Begin with the performance gap

A topic request is a starting point. Understanding the challenge behind it helps determine whether training is the right intervention and what the program should address.

Read the practice note

Useful questions include: what is happening now, what should be happening, who needs to act differently, and what else in the work environment may support or obstruct change?

05 · Human behavior

Reflection makes room for choice

Self-awareness is useful when it leads to a more considered response. Learning can help participants pause, examine a habit and choose how to act next.

Read the practice note

Activities should be respectful and relevant to adults at work. The facilitator’s role is to support reflection and learning, while keeping the focus on practical behavior and work relationships.

06 · Measurement

Agree on outcomes before delivery

Evaluation is clearer when the organization decides in advance what it wants to notice and when it expects to see it.

Read the practice note

Learning checks, participant reflection, manager observation and later follow-up answer different questions. Select measures that fit the desired outcome, practical constraints and time horizon.

From idea to intervention

Bring the question you are working through.

A focused conversation can turn a broad topic into a clearer training brief.

Useful starting points

  • What prompted the request now?
  • Who is the program for?
  • What should participants be able to do afterwards?
  • How will the organization recognize progress?
